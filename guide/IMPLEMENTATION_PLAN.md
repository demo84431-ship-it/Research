### What's Genuinely Missing (Not Just Thin)

These topics have **zero coverage** and need new chapters:

1. **Research Paradigms & Philosophy** — Ontology, epistemology, positivism, constructivism, pragmatism. Currently mentioned briefly in Ch01 §1.7 but deserves a proper chapter. A student who doesn't understand paradigms cannot justify their methodology.

2. **Theoretical & Conceptual Frameworks** — How to select, justify, and apply theory. The Part 6 skeleton (344 words) is not a chapter. This is fundamental — without theory, research is just data collection.

3. **Research Gap Identification** — 8 types of gaps, literature matrix, gap-finding strategies. The Part 6 skeleton (797 words) is an outline, not a chapter.

4. **Research Proposal Writing** — 13-section template, justification, significance. The Part 6 skeleton (698 words) is barely an introduction.

5. **Academic Writing Cohesion** — Linking words, signposts, reference chains, paragraph structure. The Part 6 skeleton (428 words) is a table, not a chapter.

6. **Tense Usage in Academic Writing** — Section-by-section guide. The Part 6 skeleton (650 words) has good tables but needs examples and depth.

7. **Grounded Theory** — A major qualitative methodology with zero coverage. Cannot claim to cover qualitative research without it.

8. **Open Science & Reproducibility** — Pre-registration, open data, open materials, replication, registered reports. This is the biggest methodological shift in psychology in the last decade. Zero coverage.

9. **Advanced Quantitative Methods** — SEM, multilevel modeling, Bayesian analysis, network analysis. Even a survey-level chapter is needed for a guide that claims to take readers to expert level.

10. **Submission & Publication** — Journal selection, peer review process, revision strategies, responding to reviewers. The Part 6 skeleton (490 words) lists 9 points but explains none.

### What Has Enough Research Already

These chapters are well-written and adequately sourced (based on content analysis):

- Ch01 (What is Research) — references Baddeley, Ebbinghaus, Braun & Clarke, Cuijpers, etc.
- Ch03 (Research Design) — references Darley & Batson, Skinner, CONSORT, Campbell & Stanley concepts
- Ch04 (Research Question) — references PICO framework, Creswell concepts
- Ch08 (Abstract) — solid examples
- Ch10 (Methods) — covers both quantitative and qualitative structures
- Ch16 (Systematic Review) — PRISMA guidelines referenced
- Ch18 (Qualitative Study) — Braun & Clarke, Smith et al. (IPA) referenced

These need **research augmentation** (new papers to cite):

- Ch05, Ch06, Ch09, Ch11-14, Ch19-22, Ch27-28, Ch34-35

## Part B: Restructured Guide — 7 Parts with Descriptive Names

### Why 7 Parts, Not 5

The current 5-part structure is too coarse. "Part 1: Foundations" bundles paradigmatic questions (what is research?) with practical setup (how do I search literature?). "Part 3: Research Designs" mixes review methods with empirical designs. A reader needs the structure to mirror their actual journey.
