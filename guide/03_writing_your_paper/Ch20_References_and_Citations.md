# Chapter 14: References and Appendices

## Opening

References and appendices are the final components of a research paper, but they are far from afterthoughts. References provide the scholarly foundation for your work — they allow readers to trace your claims back to their sources, verify your arguments, and explore related research. Appendices provide the detailed supplementary information that supports your main text without cluttering it. Together, references and appendices demonstrate thoroughness, transparency, and scholarly rigor. This chapter covers the APA 7th edition reference style, how to manage references efficiently, what belongs in appendices, and how to avoid common errors that frustrate reviewers.

---

## 14.1 The Reference List

### 14.1.1 Purpose

The reference list serves to:

- **Credit original authors** — Give proper attribution to the work you have built upon
- **Enable verification** — Allow readers to locate and read your sources
- **Demonstrate scholarship** — Show that you have engaged deeply with the literature
- **Avoid plagiarism** — Proper citation is your defense against plagiarism accusations

### 14.1.2 APA 7th Edition Rules

The APA 7th edition (2020) introduced several changes from the 6th edition. Here are the key rules:

**General format:**
> Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Title of Periodical*, *volume*(issue), page–page. https://doi.org/xxxxx

**Key rules:**

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Authors** | Last name, Initials. Use "&" before the last author. List up to 20 authors. |
| **Date** | (Year). For forthcoming papers, use "(in press)." |
| **Title** | Only first word and proper nouns capitalized (sentence case) |
| **Journal name** | Italicized, title case |
| **Volume** | Italicized |
| **Issue** | In parentheses, not italicized |
| **DOI** | Always include if available, as a hyperlink |
| **URL** | For web sources without DOI; no retrieval date needed for stable content |

### 14.1.3 Common Reference Types

**Journal article:**
> Sharma, R., & Singh, T. (2021). COVID-19 and mental health: A qualitative exploration. *Frontiers in Psychology*, *12*, 648334. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.648334

**Book:**
> Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2022). *Thematic analysis: A practical guide*. SAGE.

**Book chapter:**
> Smith, J. (2020). Mindfulness in clinical practice. In A. Jones & B. Lee (Eds.), *Handbook of clinical psychology* (pp. 123–145). Springer.

**Website:**
> World Health Organization. (2023). *Mental health and COVID-19*. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-and-covid-19

**Thesis/Dissertation:**
> Kumar, A. (2022). *Social media use and self-esteem in Indian adolescents* [Doctoral dissertation, University of Lucknow]. Shodhganga.

**Conference paper:**
> Patel, S. (2023, March 15–18). AI-powered therapy: Current status and future directions [Paper presentation]. National Conference on Psychology and Technology, New Delhi, India.

### 14.1.4 Hanging Indent and Alphabetization

- References are listed **alphabetically** by the first author's last name
- Each reference uses a **hanging indent** — the first line is flush left, subsequent lines are indented 0.5 inches
- **Do not number** references in APA style

## 14.2 In-Text Citations

### 14.2.1 Parenthetical Citation

Place the author and year in parentheses at the end of the sentence:

> Social media use is associated with increased anxiety in adolescents (Twenge et al., 2018).

### 14.2.2 Narrative Citation

Include the author's name in the sentence, with the year in parentheses:

> Twenge et al. (2018) found that social media use is associated with increased anxiety.

### 14.2.3 Multiple Authors

| Number of Authors | First Citation | Subsequent Citations |
|-------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| 1 | (Smith, 2021) | (Smith, 2021) |
| 2 | (Smith & Jones, 2021) | (Smith & Jones, 2021) |
| 3 or more | (Smith et al., 2021) | (Smith et al., 2021) |

### 14.2.4 Multiple Sources

> Several studies have found this association (Smith, 2021; Jones & Lee, 2020; Patel et al., 2019).

List multiple sources in **alphabetical order** (same as the reference list), separated by semicolons.

### 14.2.5 Secondary Sources

When you cite a source you found mentioned in another source:

> Freud's concept of the unconscious (as cited in Jones, 2020) has been influential...

In the reference list, include only the source you actually read (Jones, 2020 in this case).

## 14.3 Common Citation Mistakes

1. **Missing DOIs** — Always include DOIs for journal articles
2. **Wrong capitalization** — Article titles are sentence case; journal names are title case
3. **Missing authors** — List all authors up to 20
4. **Incorrect et al. usage** — "et al." is only for 3+ authors, and it is not italicized
5. **Citing without reading** — Never cite a paper based only on its abstract
6. **Inconsistent style** — All references must follow the same format
7. **Missing page numbers** — Include page ranges for book chapters and articles
8. **URLs without context** — Include a description of what the URL leads to

> **Pro Tip:** Use a reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) to automatically format references. But always double-check the output — automated tools make mistakes, especially with capitalization and DOIs.

## 14.4 Appendices

### 14.4.1 What Belongs in Appendices

Appendices contain supplementary material that is too detailed for the main text but necessary for transparency and reproducibility:

| Appendix | Contents |
|----------|----------|
| **A** | Survey instruments, questionnaires, interview guides |
| **B** | Informed consent form |
| **C** | Detailed statistical outputs (SPSS/R output tables) |
| **D** | Supplementary analyses (e.g., sensitivity analyses, robustness checks) |
| **E** | Coding scheme (for qualitative studies) |
| **F** | Participant recruitment materials |
| **G** | IRB approval letter |

### 14.4.2 Formatting Rules

- Label each appendix with a letter: **Appendix A**, **Appendix B**, etc.
- If you have only one appendix, label it simply **Appendix**
- Each appendix starts on a new page
- Reference each appendix in the main text: "(see Appendix A)"
- Tables and figures in appendices are numbered separately: "Table A1," "Figure B1"

### 14.4.3 What Does NOT Belong in Appendices

- **Raw data** — Provide a data availability statement instead
- **Entire books or articles** — Cite them in the references
- **Irrelevant material** — Only include material that supports your specific study
- **Your analysis code** — Share via GitHub or OSF, not in appendices (unless the journal requires it)

---

## 14.5 Supplementary Materials

Many journals now allow or require **supplementary materials** (also called online-only materials or supporting information). These are separate from appendices and are hosted online by the journal.

**Common supplementary materials:**
- Additional tables and figures
- Detailed methodological procedures
- Sensitivity analyses
- Qualitative codebooks
- Video or audio recordings (for qualitative research)
- Analysis scripts (R, Python, SPSS syntax)

> **Pro Tip:** If your main text has too many tables, move less critical ones to supplementary materials. Reviewers appreciate a clean main text with detailed supporting information available online.

---

## 14.6 Data Availability Statements

Increasingly, journals require a **data availability statement** explaining how readers can access your data. Options include:

- **Open data:** "Data are available at [repository URL]."
- **Restricted access:** "Data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request."
- **No access:** "Data are not available due to privacy/ethical restrictions."

For Indian psychology research, consider using:
- **Open Science Framework (OSF)** — Free, widely used
- **Figshare** — Free for small datasets
- **Institutional repositories** — If your university has one

> **Pro Tip:** Even if not required, sharing your data (de-identified) increases the credibility and impact of your work. It also allows others to replicate your findings.

## 14.7 Preparing for Submission

Before submitting your paper, use this checklist:

**References:**
- [ ] All in-text citations have corresponding reference list entries
- [ ] All reference list entries are cited in the text
- [ ] DOIs are included for all journal articles
- [ ] Format follows APA 7th edition exactly
- [ ] References are alphabetized
- [ ] Hanging indents are applied

**Appendices:**
- [ ] Each appendix is labeled with a letter
- [ ] Each appendix is referenced in the main text
- [ ] Survey instruments are included
- [ ] Informed consent form is included
- [ ] IRB approval is included (if applicable)

**General:**
- [ ] All tables and figures are numbered and referenced
- [ ] All abbreviations are defined at first use
- [ ] Running head is formatted correctly
- [ ] Page numbers are in the correct position
- [ ] Author information is removed for blind review (if required)

---

## Key Takeaways

- The reference list **credits sources, enables verification, and demonstrates scholarship**.
- Follow **APA 7th edition** format exactly — author names, dates, titles, DOIs, and capitalization all matter.
- Use a **reference manager** but always double-check the output.
- **Appendices** contain supplementary materials (instruments, consent forms, detailed analyses) that support the main text.
- Include a **data availability statement** — even if data cannot be shared, explain why.
- Use the **submission checklist** before every submission to avoid common errors.
- **Never cite a paper you have not read** — always access the full text.
