# Chapter 39: Tense Usage in Academic Writing

## Why This Matters

Using the wrong tense is one of the most common mistakes in academic writing. Each section of a research paper has specific tense conventions. Getting tense wrong makes your writing confusing and unprofessional.

---

## Section-by-Section Guide

### Abstract

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | State purpose, background, conclusions | "This study **examines** the role of urban green spaces in reducing heat island effects." |
| **Past** | Describe methods and results | "Data **were collected** from 30 urban zones over a 12-month period." |

### Introduction

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | Current knowledge, accepted facts | "Genomics **provides** crucial information for rational drug design." |
| **Past** | Ideas once accepted but no longer valid | "It **was believed** that ulcers **were caused** by spicy food." |

### Literature Review

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | Discussing current state of knowledge | "Research **shows** that sleep deprivation **affects** cognitive performance." |
| **Past** | Reporting specific past studies | "Smith (2020) **found** that stress **reduced** test scores by 15%." |
| **Present perfect** | Summarizing trends over time | "Several studies **have demonstrated** the effectiveness of CBT." |

### Methods

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** | Describe what you did | "Total phosphorus and total nitrogen **were measured** using standard procedures." |
| **Present** | Refer to tables/figures in the paper | "Table 1 **demonstrates** the success of cloning in various animal species." |

### Results

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** | Describe your findings | "Overall, more than 70% of the insects **collected were** non-phytophagous." |
| **Present** | Refer to figures, tables, graphs | "Figure 1 **displays** the comparative variation in morphology." |

### Discussion

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | Explain significance, implications | "Removal of vegetation **appears** to negatively affect water quality." |
| **Past** | Summarize your findings | "Leaf carbon and phenolic content **did not differ** across sites." |

### Conclusion

| Tense | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** | Summarize what you found | "The study **found** evidence of tillage and irrigation within the study area." |
| **Present** | Discuss implications, future directions | "Further studies **are necessary** to determine the effects of social media on mental health." |

---

## Quick Reference Table

| Section | Primary Tense | Secondary Tense |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Present + Past | — |
| Introduction | Present | Past |
| Literature Review | Present + Past | Present Perfect |
| Methods | Past | Present (for figures) |
| Results | Past | Present (for figures) |
| Discussion | Present | Past |
| Conclusion | Past | Present |

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mixing tenses randomly** within a paragraph
2. **Using present tense in Methods** — "We collect data" (should be "We collected data")
3. **Using past tense for current knowledge** — "Smith found that exercise improves health" (if this is current knowledge, use present: "Research shows that exercise improves health")
4. **Forgetting present tense for figures** — "Figure 1 showed" (should be "Figure 1 shows" — the figure still exists)

---

## Checklist

- [ ] My abstract uses present for purpose/conclusions and past for methods/results
- [ ] My introduction uses present for current knowledge
- [ ] My methods section uses past tense throughout
- [ ] My results section uses past for findings, present for figures
- [ ] My discussion uses present for implications, past for summarizing findings
- [ ] I have not mixed tenses randomly within paragraphs
