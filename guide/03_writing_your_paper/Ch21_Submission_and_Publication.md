# Chapter 45: Submission and Publication

## Why This Matters

A great paper that nobody reads is a wasted paper. This chapter covers the 9 things to check before submission, how to choose the right journal, and how to respond to reviewers.

---

## 9 Things to Check Before Submission

### 1. Journal Fit
Match your topic, article type, and contribution to the journal's scope and readership. Read the journal's aims and scope. Check if they publish your type of study.

### 2. Author Guidelines
Check formatting, word limits, reference style, figure rules, and required files. Every journal has different requirements.

### 3. Reporting Checklist
Use the right guideline for your study type:
- **CONSORT** — Randomized controlled trials
- **PRISMA** — Systematic reviews
- **STROBE** — Observational studies
- **STARD** — Diagnostic accuracy studies
- **COREQ** — Qualitative interviews

### 4. Searchable Title
Make the title clear about the topic, study design, and main contribution. Avoid vague titles.

### 5. Strong Abstract
Ensure the abstract reflects the question, methods, main results, and conclusion.

### 6. Methods and Statistics
Check for missing details, unclear variables, weak sample rationale, or unsuitable analyses.

### 7. References and Citations
Verify that citations are accurate, current, complete, and correctly formatted.

### 8. Ethics and Disclosures
Include ethics approval, consent, funding, conflicts, author roles, and data/code notes.

### 9. Submission Package
Proofread the cover letter, figures, tables, supplements, and portal metadata.

---

## 8 Reasons Your Paper Isn't Getting Cited

1. **Title is too vague** — Make the main concept, population, method, or outcome clear
2. **Abstract misses searchable terms** — Include key concepts, variables, population, region, theory
3. **Keywords are too generic** — Use field-specific phrases, not broad terms
4. **Poor access limits citations** — Share a legal version through a repository
5. **No clear link to the debate** — Show the gap and contribution clearly
6. **The paper is hard to read** — Dense writing reduces reuse
7. **Methods are not reusable** — Make design, data, and measures transparent
8. **Wrong journal audience** — Choose venues for audience fit, not only impact factor

## Responding to Reviewers

### Do:
- Respond to every comment
- Be polite and professional
- Explain what you changed and why
- If you disagree, provide evidence
- Thank the reviewers

### Don't:
- Ignore comments
- Be defensive or dismissive
- Make changes without explaining them
- Take criticism personally

## Checklist

- [ ] Paper matches journal scope
- [ ] All formatting requirements are met
- [ ] Reporting checklist is completed
- [ ] Title is searchable and specific
- [ ] Abstract covers question, methods, results, conclusion
- [ ] References are complete and correctly formatted
- [ ] Ethics and disclosures are included
- [ ] Cover letter is written
- [ ] All figures and tables are high quality
