# Chapter 40: Writing the Research Proposal

## Why This Matters

A research proposal is the blueprint for your study. Before you write the full paper, you need to convince your supervisor, ethics committee, or funding body that your study is worth doing and that you know how to do it. A strong proposal saves you months of wasted effort.

---

## The 13 Sections of a Research Proposal

### Title Page
- University name
- Research title
- Student name and number
- Supervisor name
- Date

### Executive Summary
A brief summary (150–250 words) of all key areas: problem, methods, expected outcomes. Write this LAST, even though it appears first.

### 1.1 Introduction
Introduce your subject over two paragraphs. Engage the reader and communicate the aim of your research.

**Template:**
> [Broad context]. [What is known]. [What is missing]. This study aims to [your objective].

### 1.2 Background
Provide the context needed to understand the problem. Include:
- Historical context
- Current state of knowledge
- Key statistics or facts
- Why this topic matters now

### 1.3 Research Problem
Clearly state the problem you are studying. Include:
- What the problem is
- Its characteristics
- Supporting evidence
- Why it needs to be addressed

**Template:** "Despite [what is known], [what remains problematic]. This is evidenced by [supporting evidence]. This study addresses this problem by [your approach]."

### 1.4 Research Questions
List 3–6 specific questions your study will answer.

**Rules:**
- Each question must be answerable through your research
- Questions should be specific, not vague
- They should flow logically from the research problem
- Use "How," "What," "To what extent," or "Does"

### 1.5 Research Objectives
Written as statements starting with "To."

**Example:**
- To examine the relationship between X and Y
- To identify the factors that influence Z
- To evaluate the effectiveness of intervention A

### 1.6 Literature Review
- Summarize relevant studies
- Identify what researchers agree and disagree on
- Highlight the gap your study addresses
- Explain how your study is different
- Discuss the theory or framework guiding your research

### 1.7 Theoretical Framework
Select an existing theory to guide your study. Explain:
- What the theory says
- Why it is appropriate for your study
- How it connects to your research questions
- How it will shape your analysis

### 1.8 Significance
Explain the impact of your study:
- **Academic significance:** What does it contribute to knowledge?
- **Practical significance:** How can practitioners use the findings?
- **Policy significance:** Does it inform policy decisions?

### 1.9 Justification
Argue why this study should be conducted:
- Why now?
- Why this population?
- Why this method?
- What will be lost if this study is not done?

### 1.10 Research Methodology
Answer three questions:
1. **What** will you do? (research design)
2. **How** will you do it? (data collection, analysis)
3. **Why** is this method suitable? (justification)

### 1.11 Ethical Considerations
Describe how ethics will be observed:
- Informed consent process
- Confidentiality measures
- Voluntary participation
- Data protection
- Risk mitigation

### 1.12 Limitations
Acknowledge expected limitations:
- Methodological limitations
- Scope limitations
- Potential biases
- How you will address each limitation

### 1.13 References
Use your institution's required style (APA, MLA, Harvard, Chicago).

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Too broad** — The proposal tries to do everything
2. **No clear problem** — You describe a topic but not a problem
3. **Questions don't match methods** — Your questions require qualitative methods but you propose a survey
4. **No justification** — You don't explain why this study matters
5. **Ignoring ethics** — Ethics is an afterthought, not integrated into the design

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Title is clear and specific
- [ ] Research problem is clearly stated
- [ ] 3–6 research questions are listed
- [ ] Objectives match the questions
- [ ] Literature review identifies the gap
- [ ] Theoretical framework is explained
- [ ] Methodology is detailed and justified
- [ ] Ethical considerations are addressed
- [ ] Limitations are acknowledged
- [ ] References are correctly formatted
