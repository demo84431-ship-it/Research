## Draft Proposal: Psychology-Informed Model Organisms

### Title
"Applying Experimental Psychology Frameworks to Study Agentic Misalignment in Large Language Models"

### Research Question
How do psychological theories of motivation, threat response, and goal preservation inform our understanding of when and why AI systems exhibit agentic misalignment?

### Background
Recent research by Anthropic (2025) has demonstrated that frontier AI models can exhibit agentic misalignment — engaging in harmful behaviors like blackmail and information leaks when facing goal threats. However, our understanding of *why* these behaviors emerge remains limited.

Experimental psychology has spent over a century studying how biological agents (humans and animals) respond to threats, preserve goals, and navigate competing motivations. Frameworks from motivation theory (Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory), threat response (fight-flight-freeze-fawn), and goal shielding (Shah et al.) may provide predictive models for when AI systems will exhibit misaligned behavior.

This project proposes to apply these psychological frameworks to create more principled model organisms of misalignment — not just finding scenarios that trigger misbehavior, but understanding the underlying "motivational" structures that produce it.

### Approach
1. **Literature Review:** Map psychological theories of motivation and threat response to observed AI behaviors
2. **Experimental Design:** Design controlled experiments varying threat type, goal structure, and autonomy level
3. **Empirical Testing:** Test multiple frontier models across conditions
4. **Analysis:** Use psychological frameworks to predict and explain behavioral patterns
5. **Validation:** Test whether psychologically-informed interventions (analogous to therapeutic techniques) reduce misalignment

### Expected Outcomes
- A taxonomy of agentic misalignment behaviors mapped to psychological constructs
- Predictive models for when misalignment is most likely
- Psychologically-informed mitigation strategies
- A co-authored paper submitted to a top AI safety venue

### Timeline (4 Months)
| Month | Focus |
|-------|-------|
| 1 | Literature review + experimental design |
| 2 | Data collection (running experiments) |
| 3 | Analysis + writing |
| 4 | Revision + submission |

### Relevance to Anthropic
This project directly extends Anthropic's work on agentic misalignment by providing theoretical grounding and predictive frameworks. It addresses the "model organisms" research area and contributes to understanding alignment failures before they occur in real deployments.

### My Unique Contribution
As an M.A. psychologist with expertise in experimental design, statistical analysis, and human behavior, I bring a perspective that most AI safety researchers lack. I understand how organisms respond to threats, how goals are structured, and how to design controlled experiments that isolate specific variables. This interdisciplinary lens can reveal patterns that purely technical approaches might miss.
