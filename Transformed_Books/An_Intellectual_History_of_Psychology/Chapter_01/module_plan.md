## Module Plan (3 Modules)

### Module 1: The Culture of Knowledge
**Source pages:** 1–3 | **Source words:** ~1,800 | **Concepts:** 4

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Psychology is not "young" — its subject matter is as old as reflection | Opening |
| 2 | The "culture of knowledge" — intellectual traditions transcend boundaries | Opening |
| 3 | Why studying history is essential to understanding psychology | Opening + Collingwood |

**Narrative arc:** Opens with the common claim that psychology is "young" → challenges it → introduces the culture of knowledge → Collingwood's insight that history is a science of explanation, not just dates → knowing that Wundt founded a lab in 1879 is a fact, not an explanation.

### Module 2: The Historian's Biases
**Source pages:** 4–8 | **Source words:** ~2,200 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Vico's warning against revering antiquity | Vico section |
| 2 | Scholars are not representative of their age | Vico section |
| 3 | Eyewitness accounts are not automatically reliable | Vico section |
| 4 | Scholastica successionis — the error of assuming ideas spread linearly | Vico section |
| 5 | History is not cyclical — recurring patterns ≠ identical conditions | Ages section |

**Narrative arc:** Vico's five biases → the most dangerous one: scholastica successionis (assuming ideas ripple from one source) → the Renaissance example (was it really a "rebirth"?) → history is not cyclical, even when it looks like it.

**Key terms to define:** scholastica successionis, Zeitgeist, presentist history, contextualist history

**Research anchors:** Vico's *The New Science* (1725) — foundational historiographic work

### Module 3: Psychology's Ancient Foundations
**Source pages:** 8–13 | **Source words:** ~1,800 | **Concepts:** 4

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | History requires human agency — not all events are "historical" | History as explanation |
| 2 | The "great man" theory vs broader human agency | History as explanation |
| 3 | Psychology young as a science, old as a subject | Final section |
| 4 | Collingwood's critique: psychology's mistake was mimicking physics | Final section |

**Narrative arc:** What makes an event "historical"? → human agency, not great individuals → psychology's subject matter is ancient (Aristotle studied perception, emotion, behavior) → but it's young as a scientific discipline → Collingwood's bombshell: psychology's chief mistake was identifying with the physical sciences → the foundations are philosophical, and that's where the book begins.

**Key terms to define:** human agency, great man theory, scientia, craft vs science

**Research anchors:** Collingwood's critique of psychology — well-documented in philosophy of science literature
