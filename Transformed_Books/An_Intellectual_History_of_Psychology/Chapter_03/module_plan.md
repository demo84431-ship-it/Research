# Chapter 3: The Hellenistic Age — Module Plan

> **Source:** An Intellectual History of Psychology (3rd Edition), Daniel N. Robinson
> **Chapter:** 3 — The Hellenistic Age: Aristotle, the Epicureans, and the Stoics
> **Source words:** ~12,600
> **Estimated modules:** 7

---

## Module 1: Aristotle's Revolution
**Source sections:** Aristotle's Philosophical Development, Aristotle on the Soul
**Source words:** ~2,400
**Concepts (5):**
1. Aristotle's three developmental stages (Jaeger's framework)
2. Departure from Plato — from dialectical to empirical method
3. De Anima — the soul as biological, not metaphysical
4. Faculties of the soul (nutritive, perceptive, locomotor, universalizing)
5. The "common sense" (koine aisthesis) — sensory integration

**Narrative arc:** Aristotle enters the Academy as a Platonist → gradually breaks away → builds a psychology grounded in biology, not ideas → the soul is not a prisoner of the body but its living function.

**Key terms to define:** De Anima, koine aisthesis, epistemonikon, teleological

**Research anchors:** Jaeger's developmental thesis; modern neuroscience on sensory integration (if useful)

---

## Module 2: Learning, Memory, and the Blank Slate
**Source sections:** Aristotle on Learning and Memory, Aristotle on the Problem of Knowledge (first half)
**Source words:** ~2,000
**Concepts (5):**
1. Associationism — repetition as the source of memory strength
2. The "pleasure principle" in learning and morality
3. Mind as a blank tablet (tabula rasa precursor — 429b-430a)
4. The ten categories of knowledge
5. Knowing that vs. knowing why

**Narrative arc:** Aristotle proposes the first systematic theory of learning → memory is biological, not mystical → the mind starts empty and is filled by experience → but knowledge requires more than perception.

**Key terms to define:** associationism, prolepsis (briefly, as contrast), categories

**Research anchors:** Modern memory research on repetition and association (Ebbinghaus curve, if useful)

---

## Module 3: How Do We Know Anything?
**Source sections:** Aristotle on the Problem of Knowledge (second half), Aristotle on Universals
**Source words:** ~2,000
**Concepts (5):**
1. The problem of universals — how do we know "cat" from seeing a cat?
2. Demonstrative knowledge vs. mere perception
3. The syllogism — reasoning from general to particular
4. Nativism vs. empiricism — Aristotle's compromise
5. The mind constructs universals from sensory data

**Narrative arc:** The child learns to say "cat" → but how does the mind go from particular cats to the universal concept? → Aristotle's solution: the senses provide data, the mind extracts universals → neither pure nativism nor pure empiricism.

**Key terms to define:** universals, syllogism, demonstration

**Research anchors:** Modern concept formation research (prototype theory, Eleanor Rosch)

---

## Module 4: Aristotle's Ethical Psychology
**Source sections:** Aristotle and Platonism, The Problem of Conduct
**Source words:** ~2,400
**Concepts (5):**
1. Dynamic psychological development (vs. Plato's two-stage model)
2. The four causes (material, formal, efficient, final)
3. Teleological explanation in nature and behavior
4. Virtue as a mean between excess and deficiency
5. Habit and education shape character (ethos = habit)

**Narrative arc:** Aristotle rejects Plato's binary model → psychology is developmental → behavior is explained by its purpose (final cause) → virtue is not innate but formed by habit → the golden mean.

**Key terms to define:** teleology, final cause, hexis, golden mean

**Research anchors:** Modern habit formation research; virtue ethics revival (if useful)

---

## Module 5: The Aristotelian Legacy
**Source sections:** The Aristotelian Legacy, Stoic and Epicurean Psychology (introduction)
**Source words:** ~1,800
**Concepts (5):**
1. Aristotle's enduring influence on psychology and science
2. The shift from classical Athens to the Hellenistic world
3. Stoicism and Epicureanism as responses to Cynics and Skeptics
4. Both systems based on natural-science perspective (physicalism/monism)
5. Materialism becomes respectable in the new political climate

**Narrative arc:** Aristotle's legacy is enormous but complex → the political world changes (Alexander, empire) → new philosophies emerge to answer the Skeptics → Stoicism and Epicureanism both ground ethics in nature.

**Key terms to define:** Hellenistic, monism, logos

**Research anchors:** None critical (contextual/transition module)

---

## Module 6: The Physics of the Soul — Epicurean and Stoic Psychology
**Source sections:** Stoic and Epicurean Psychology (main body — knowledge and sensation)
**Source words:** ~2,000
**Concepts (5):**
1. Epicurean psychology — soul as material (fire, wind, air, unnamed element)
2. All knowledge originates in sensation (Epicurus)
3. Stoic psychology — mental image (phantasia) as starting point
4. Both systems: associationism → general concepts from elementary sensations
5. Stoic criteria of validity — clarity and utility (statistical test)

**Narrative arc:** Both rival schools start from sensation → but differ on what lies beneath → Epicureans: atoms and void → Stoics: rational fire (logos) → both arrive at similar epistemologies despite different foundations.

**Key terms to define:** phantasia, katalepseis, apatheia

**Research anchors:** Modern debates on empiricism vs. nativism in concept formation

---

## Module 7: The Ethics of Resignation — Stoic and Epicurean Conduct
**Source sections:** Stoic and Epicurean Psychology (ethics and conduct), closing sections
**Source words:** ~2,000
**Concepts (5):**
1. Epicurean ethics — pleasure, moderation, withdrawal (ataraxia/apatheia)
2. Stoic ethics — duty (kathekon), rationality, reconciliation to destiny
3. The "pleasure principle" as shared foundation
4. Pathos as disease of the soul (Stoic view of emotion)
5. Roman adoption — law, cosmopolitanism, human rights, and eventual decline

**Narrative arc:** Both schools seek happiness through nature → Epicureans retreat, Stoics engage → both renounce passion → Rome adopts both → Stoicism gives us "human rights" → but neither can save the empire.

**Key terms to define:** ataraxia, kathekon, pathos, oikos

**Research anchors:** Modern positive psychology and Stoic revival (if useful)
