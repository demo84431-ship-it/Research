# Module Plan — Chapter 12: Contemporary Formulations

**Source:** `books/An_Intellectual_History_of_Psychology/markdown/16_Ch12_Contemporary_Formulations.md`
**Source words:** ~19,565
**Planned modules:** 10

---

## Module 1: Method as Metaphysic
- **Source lines:** Opening through end of Helmholtz section (pp. 1–8)
- **Concepts:** Psychology as 19th-century "footnote"; no single scientific method; method as metaphysical commitment; Victorian romanticism vs. science; Helmholtz on Hegelianism
- **Key terms:** scholastica successionis civitatium, method as metaphysic
- **Narrative arc:** Opens with the claim that contemporary psychology is still 19th-century psychology. Unpacks the idea that choosing a method is choosing a metaphysics. Victorian England as battleground between romanticism and science.

## Module 2: Thorndike and the Roots of Behaviorism
- **Source lines:** Titchener/James section through Thorndike's laws (pp. 8–14)
- **Concepts:** Titchener's introspection; James's consciousness; shift from structuralism to behaviorism; Thorndike's law of effect and law of exercise; Pavlov's influence
- **Key terms:** law of effect, law of exercise, conditioned reflex
- **Narrative arc:** The decline of introspection and the rise of objective methods. Thorndike's experiments with cats and his provisional laws of learning. Pavlov's conditioning as the new unit of behavior.

## Module 3: Watson's Behaviorism
- **Source lines:** Watson's manifesto through reception (pp. 14–17)
- **Concepts:** Watson's 1913 manifesto; rejection of consciousness; stimulus substitution; rejection of instincts; conditioned reflex as unit of behavior; behaviorism's reception
- **Key terms:** behaviorism, stimulus substitution
- **Narrative arc:** Watson's revolutionary declaration. His program: prediction and control of behavior, no mental states. The conditioned reflex as building block. Why behaviorism caught on in America.

## Module 4: Skinner's Operant Revolution
- **Source lines:** Skinner section through schedules of reinforcement (pp. 17–22)
- **Concepts:** Skinner vs. Watson; operant vs. respondent behavior; positive/negative reinforcers; law of effect stripped of mentalism; schedules of reinforcement; descriptive science
- **Key terms:** operant conditioning, positive reinforcer, negative reinforcer, schedules of reinforcement
- **Narrative arc:** Skinner's more sophisticated behaviorism. The operant as the unit. Reinforcement schedules and their surprising effects. Behaviorism as descriptive science, not theory.

## Module 5: The Skinnerian Worldview
- **Source lines:** "Autonomous man" section through end of Skinner (pp. 20–22)
- **Concepts:** Attack on "autonomous man"; reasons vs. causes; freedom and dignity as verbal operants; behavior modification; critics from humanists, ethicists, philosophers
- **Key terms:** autonomous man, behavior modification
- **Narrative arc:** Skinner's philosophical radicalism. Why he attacked "autonomous man." The behaviorist rejoinder to critics. Practical applications from clinics to classrooms. The controversy.

## Module 6: Gestalt Psychology — The Continental Reply
- **Source lines:** Gestalt section opening through phi phenomenon (pp. 23–26)
- **Concepts:** Wertheimer, Koffka, Kohler; phi phenomenon; perception as mental achievement; isomorphism; Kantian-Hegelian roots; Gestalt critique of behaviorism
- **Key terms:** Gestalt psychology, phi phenomenon, isomorphism, Gestalt-Qualität
- **Narrative arc:** The Continental alternative to behaviorism. The phi phenomenon as demonstration that perception is not stimulus-bound. Isomorphism: brain structure matches perceptual structure.

## Module 7: Kohler's Apes and Tolman's Rats
- **Source lines:** Kohler's studies through Tolman/latent learning (pp. 25–28)
- **Concepts:** Kohler's insight experiments; stacking boxes, joining sticks; Tolman's cognitive maps; latent learning; transposition; learning vs. performance
- **Key terms:** insight, latent learning, transposition, cognitive maps
- **Narrative arc:** Kohler's chimpanzees demonstrate insight — not trial-and-error. Tolman's rats learn without reward. Both challenge the law of effect. What was learned: relationships, not stimuli.

## Module 8: Gestalt Principles and Legacy
- **Source lines:** Gestalt theses 1–5 through Piaget (pp. 27–28)
- **Concepts:** Transactions with environment; effective stimulus from organism's POV; perceptual organization laws; shape constancy; naive phenomenalism rejected; Piaget's inheritance
- **Key terms:** shape constancy, grouping, effective stimulus
- **Narrative arc:** The five Gestalt theses. Perception transforms, not copies. Shape constancy as proof. Legacy in Piaget, psycholinguistics, cognitive science.

## Module 9: Physiological Psychology — Pavlov and Lashley
- **Source lines:** Physiological Psychology section (pp. 29–32)
- **Concepts:** Pavlov as physiologist, not behaviorist; conditioning, extinction, generalization, irradiation; Lashley's mass action and equipotentiality; eye-patch experiment; engram search
- **Key terms:** mass action, equipotentiality, engram, stimulus generalization
- **Narrative arc:** Pavlov's biological psychology — more than just conditioning. Lashley's elegant experiments demolishing simple associationism. The brain as dynamic whole, not telephone switchboard.

## Module 10: Defining Psychology
- **Source lines:** Defining Psychology section + Notes (pp. 33–36)
- **Concepts:** Psychology repeats historical patterns; science and its limitations; mind-body problem persists; psychology as history of ideas; autonomous man and free will
- **Key terms:** autonomous man (revisited)
- **Narrative arc:** The concluding reflection. Psychology's essential tensions — Nature vs. Spirit, method vs. substance — are perennial. The discipline's history IS its subject matter.
