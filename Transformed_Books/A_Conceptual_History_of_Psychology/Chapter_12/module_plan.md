## Module Plan (5 Modules)

### Module 1: The Machine That Thinks — Information Theory and the Birth of Cognitive Science
**Source lines:** 1–300 | **Source words:** ~3,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Shannon's communication theory — bit, channel capacity, source-destination model | Shannon |
| 2 | Wiener's cybernetics — feedback, servomechanisms, purposive machines, "information is not matter or energy" | Wiener |
| 3 | Broadbent's information processing — selective attention, limited capacity channel, perception vs. stimulus | Broadbent |
| 4 | Newell & Simon — computer simulation, Logic Theory Machine, General Problem Solver, rules and representations | Newell & Simon |
| 5 | Human factors research — WWII origins, radar operators, cockpit design, information processing demands | WWII |

**Narrative arc:** WWII produces new technologies (radar, torpedoes, computers) → Shannon develops mathematical theory of communication → Wiener shows machines can be purposive → Broadbent applies information processing to human attention → Newell & Simon simulate human problem-solving on computers → the mind-as-computer metaphor is born.

**Key terms to define:** information theory, bit, cybernetics, feedback, information processing, selective attention, computer simulation, artificial intelligence

**Research anchors:**
- Shannon, C. E. (1948). "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" — foundational information theory
- Newell, A., & Simon, H. A. (1958). "Heuristic Problem Solving" — computer simulation of cognition

### Module 3: Was It Really a Revolution? Paradigm Shifts and Theoretical Meaning
**Source lines:** 700–1050 | **Source words:** ~3,500 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Kuhn's paradigm shift — was the cognitive revolution a Kuhnian revolution? (no) | Kuhn |
| 2 | Behaviorism survived — radical behaviorism expanded institutionally during the same period | Krantz |
| 3 | From intervening variables to hypothetical constructs — operational definition vs. independent theoretical meaning | MacCorquodale & Meehl |
| 4 | Cognitive content — neobehaviorist "cognition" lacked genuine representational content | Hull, Osgood |
| 5 | Strong continuity — behaviorists denied cognitive processes are distinct from reflexive processes; cognitive psychologists affirmed it | Morgan |

**Narrative arc:** Many pioneers saw themselves as Kuhnian revolutionaries → but behaviorism did not die — it survived alongside cognitive psychology → the real change was in how theories were conceived — from operationally defined intervening variables to independently meaningful hypothetical constructs → neobehaviorist "cognitive" terms lacked genuine representational content → the cognitive revolution was not a paradigm shift but a genuine theoretical discontinuity.

**Key terms to define:** paradigm shift, intervening variable, hypothetical construct, operational definition, strong continuity, explanatory fiction

**Research anchors:**
- Kuhn, T. S. (1962/1970). *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* — the framework everyone was reading
- MacCorquodale, K., & Meehl, P. E. (1948). "On a Distinction Between Hypothetical Constructs and Intervening Variables" — the key theoretical distinction

### Module 4: The Mind Unbound — Cognition as Its Own Subject
**Source lines:** 1050–1300 | **Source words:** ~2,500 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Cognitive states as objects of explanation — not just explaining behavior, but explaining cognition itself | Neisser |
| 2 | Not structuralism — cognitive psychology ≠ Titchener; cognition need not be imagistic or conscious | Titchener comparison |
| 3 | Not anthropomorphism — Morgan's canon misinterpreted; legitimate to ascribe cognition to animals | Morgan, Griffin |
| 4 | The cognitive tradition — Würzburg school, Selz, de Groot, continuous thread from 1900s to 1950s | Würzburg |
| 5 | Neisser's ecological turn — Cognition and Reality (1976), criticism of artificiality, but not rejection of cognitive psychology | Neisser |

**Narrative arc:** Cognitive psychologists did not just replace behaviorist explanations of behavior — they made cognition itself a legitimate object of study → this was not a return to Titchener's structuralism (cognition need not be conscious or imagistic) → it was not anthropomorphism (Morgan's canon was misinterpreted) → the cognitive tradition has roots going back to the Würzburg school → even Neisser's later ecological criticism did not reject cognitive psychology, only its artificiality.

**Key terms to define:** cognitive processing, structuralism (as critique), anthropomorphism (as critique), Morgan's canon, ecological validity, cognitive tradition

**Research anchors:**
- Neisser, U. (1976). *Cognition and Reality* — ecological critique of cognitive psychology
- Bartlett, F. C. (1932). *Remembering* — early cognitive psychology of memory

### Module 5: Connections and Open Questions — From Cognitive Science to the Future
**Source lines:** 1300–1867 + synthesis | **Source words:** ~5,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Connectionism — neural networks, parallel distributed processing, Rumelhart & McClelland | Connectionism |
| 2 | The architecture of cognition — Anderson's ACT, Fodor's modularity, Marr's vision | Anderson, Fodor, Marr |
| 3 | Fragmentation and integration — cognitive psychology as a unified field? Multiple paradigms? | Jenkins, Newell |
| 4 | The autonomy of cognitive explanation — information is information, not matter or energy | Wiener, Broadbent |
| 5 | Open questions — consciousness, embodied cognition, the hard problem | Synthesis |

**Narrative arc:** Cognitive psychology faces its own internal tensions → connectionism offers an alternative to classical symbolic processing → the field fragments into multiple paradigms (symbolic, connectionist, embodied) → but the core insight endures: cognitive states and processes are real and deserve scientific study → the autonomy of cognitive explanation is preserved → open questions remain about consciousness, embodiment, and the relationship between mind and brain.

**Key terms to define:** connectionism, neural network, parallel distributed processing, modularity, embodied cognition

**Research anchors:**
- Rumelhart, D. E., & McClelland, J. L. (1986). *Parallel Distributed Processing* — connectionist revolution
- Fodor, J. A. (1983). *The Modularity of Mind* — modularity hypothesis
