## Module Plan (5 Modules)

### Module 1: From Chains to Dignity — The Reform of Asylums
**Source lines:** 1–537 | **Source words:** ~5,500 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | History of psychological disorders — ancient treatments, moral insanity, alienists, psychiatry | Opening |
| 2 | Asylum conditions — Bedlam, Hôpital Général, incarceration of the insane with criminals and destitute | Asylum history |
| 3 | Moral management — Tuke (York Retreat), Pinel (Bicêtre, Salpêtrière), Chiarugi, Daquin | Reform |
| 4 | American reform — Rush, Todd, Dix, Woodward, Earle, non-restraint movement | American reform |
| 5 | Limits of reform — overpopulation, abandonment of moral management, custodial care | Decline |

**Narrative arc:** Ancient peoples recognized psychological disorders and treated them humanely → asylums degenerated into places of incarceration → Enlightenment ideals inspired reform → Tuke, Pinel, and others championed moral management → Dorothea Dix carried reform across America → but the sheer number of inmates overwhelmed the system.

**Key terms to define:** asylum, moral management, alienist, psychiatry, neurosis, moral insanity, non-restraint movement

**Research anchors:**
- Pinel, P. (1801). *Traité médico-philosophique sur l'aliénation mentale* — foundational asylum reform
- Scull, A. (1989). *Social Order/Mental Disorder: Anglo-American Psychiatry in Historical Perspective* — critical history of asylum reform

### Module 3: Naming the Disorders — Kraepelin, Classification, and the Birth of Diagnosis
**Source lines:** 941–1256 | **Source words:** ~3,500 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Kraepelin's classification — dementia praecox, manic-depressive psychosis, course and outcome | Kraepelin |
| 2 | Bleuler's schizophrenia — disassociation of thought and emotion, treatable | Bleuler |
| 3 | DSM — from theoretical etiology to symptom-based classification, DSM I through IV | DSM |
| 4 | Meyer's psychobiological reactions — functional disorders, maladaptive learning, mental hygiene | Meyer |
| 5 | Behavior therapy — Wolpe, Eysenck, systematic desensitization, conditioning-based treatment | Behavior therapy |

**Narrative arc:** Kraepelin develops the first scientific classification of psychological disorders → Bleuler renames dementia praecox as schizophrenia → the DSM evolves from etiology-based to symptom-based classification → Meyer argues that disorders are functional reactions, not brain diseases → behavior therapists apply conditioning principles to treat disorders → the scientist-practitioner model is born.

**Key terms to define:** dementia praecox, schizophrenia, manic-depressive psychosis, DSM, psychobiological reaction, mental hygiene, behavior therapy, systematic desensitization

**Research anchors:**
- Kraepelin, E. (1883). *Compendium der Psychiatrie* — foundational classification
- Wolpe, J. (1958). *Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition* — behavior therapy foundations

### Module 4: The Professionalization of Clinical Psychology
**Source lines:** 1257–1518 | **Source words:** ~3,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | WWII and clinical psychology — VA system, demand for therapists, training programs | WWII |
| 2 | Boulder Conference (1949) — scientist-practitioner model, clinical psychology as science + practice | Boulder |
| 3 | Maslow — hierarchy of needs, self-actualization, peak experiences, humanistic motivation | Maslow |
| 4 | Rogers — client-centered therapy, unconditional positive regard, empathic understanding, genuineness | Rogers |
| 5 | Existential psychology — May, Bugental, authenticity, meaning, phenomenological approach | Existential |

**Narrative arc:** WWII creates massive demand for mental health professionals → the VA funds clinical psychology training → the Boulder Conference establishes the scientist-practitioner model → but not everyone agrees that clinical psychology should be purely scientific → Maslow proposes a humanistic alternative focused on growth and self-actualization → Rogers develops client-centered therapy → May and Bugental import existential philosophy into psychology → the "third force" is born.

**Key terms to define:** scientist-practitioner model, Boulder Conference, hierarchy of needs, self-actualization, client-centered therapy, unconditional positive regard, existential psychology, third force

**Research anchors:**
- Rogers, C. R. (1951). *Client-Centered Therapy* — foundational humanistic therapy
- Maslow, A. H. (1954). *Motivation and Personality* — hierarchy of needs
