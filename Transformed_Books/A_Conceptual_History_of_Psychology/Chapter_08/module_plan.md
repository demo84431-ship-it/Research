### Module 1: Before the Laboratory — Herbart and the Dream of a Mathematical Psychology
**Source lines:** 1–138 | **Source words:** ~2,200 | **Concepts:** 5 | **Status:** ✅ Complete

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | German university system — hospitable to new disciplines, philosophy chairs | Opening |
| 2 | Herbart's dynamic psychology — ideas as forces, attractive/repulsive, dynamic equilibrium | Herbart |
| 3 | Apperceptive mass — focused attention, repression of ideas below threshold, ideas "pop" into mind | Herbart |
| 4 | Homeostatic motivation (Drobisch) — disequilibrium as unpleasant, motivation to regain equilibrium | Drobisch |
| 5 | Educational psychology — assimilation, accommodation, Piaget anticipation | Herbart |

**Narrative arc:** Psychology existed as a philosophical subject long before Wundt → Herbart tried to make it mathematical → he treated ideas as forces with attractive and repulsive properties → ideas can be repressed below consciousness → Drobisch added homeostatic motivation → Herbart applied all this to education → his theory dominated German psychology when Wundt arrived at Leipzig.

**Key terms to define:** apperceptive mass, threshold of consciousness, repression, homeostatic motivation, assimilation, accommodation
