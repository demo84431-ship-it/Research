## Module Plan (7 Modules)

### Module 1: Before Darwin — Evolution's Early Prophets
**Source pages:** 266–268 | **Source words:** ~4,600 | **Concepts:** 5 | **Status:** ✅ Complete

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | The scala naturae — immutable hierarchy, great chain of being, fixed human nature | Opening |
| 2 | Erasmus Darwin — Zoonomia, fluid materialism, habits inherited, "ghost story" | Early theories |
| 3 | Lamarck — inheritance of acquired characteristics, innate drive to perfection, progressive development | Lamarck |
| 4 | Chalmers/Vestiges — embryonic development as model for evolution, divine plan, controversy | Chalmers |
| 5 | Lyell & Malthus — geological time (100 million years), population growth, struggle for existence | Lyell/Malthus |

**Narrative arc:** The medieval world believed in a fixed hierarchy of nature → Erasmus Darwin said habits can be inherited → Lamarck said organisms drive toward perfection and pass on acquired traits → Chalmers linked evolution to embryonic development → Lyell gave evolution enough time → Malthus gave evolution its engine: the struggle for existence.

**Key terms to define:** scala naturae, great chain of being, inheritance of acquired characteristics, fluid materialism, struggle for existence, soft heredity

**Research anchors (to search):**
- Gould, S. J. (1977). *Ontogeny and Phylogeny* — on recapitulation and the scala naturae legacy
- Burkhardt, R. W. (1977). *The Spirit of System: Lamarck and Evolutionary Biology* — definitive Lamarck study

### Module 2: Spencer's Universe — Evolution as Cosmic Law
**Source pages:** 268–275 | **Source words:** ~7,600 | **Concepts:** 5 | **Status:** ✅ Complete

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Spencer's cosmic evolution — persistence of force, homogeneity → heterogeneity, differentiation + integration | Spencer's theory |
| 2 | Survival of the fittest — Spencer coined the phrase, Malthusian struggle, progressive development | Spencer's theory |
| 3 | Social Darwinism — laissez-faire, opposition to welfare, "reform Darwinism," Protestant work ethic | Social Darwinism |

| 5 | Spencer-Bain principle — behavior followed by success tends to be repeated, conditioned reflexes anticipation | Spencer's psychology |

**Narrative arc:** Spencer sees evolution as a cosmic force driving everything from nebulae to civilizations → he coins "survival of the fittest" → he applies it to society: no welfare, no intervention, let the unfit perish → but he also integrates associationism and evolution: instincts are inherited habits → the Spencer-Bain principle anticipates operant conditioning.

**Key terms to define:** cosmic evolution, persistence of force, survival of the fittest, social Darwinism, laissez-faire, reform Darwinism, strong continuity, Spencer-Bain principle

**Research anchors (to search):**
- Hofstadter, R. (1955). *Social Darwinism in American Thought* — classic on Spencer's influence

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|

### Module 7: Minds Across Species — Instinct, Intelligence, and Morgan's Canon
**Source pages:** 273–287 | **Source words:** ~11,200 | **Concepts:** 5 | **Status:** ✅ Complete

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Spalding — sensory isolation, instinct vs. learning, critical periods, imprinting anticipation | Spalding |
| 2 | Romanes — animal intelligence, introspection by analogy, anecdotal data, anthropomorphism | Romanes |
| 3 | Morgan's canon — Occam's razor for psychology, simpler explanations preferred, Toby the dog | Morgan |
| 4 | Emergent evolution — strong discontinuity, perception of relations, abstract thought as emergent | Morgan |
| 5 | Stimulus-response psychology — strong continuity reversed (explain humans via animals), Loeb, behaviorist legacy | S-R psychology |

**Narrative arc:** Spalding isolates newborn chicks and discovers instinct → Romanes collects anecdotes of animal intelligence and attributes human-like reasoning to dogs and crows → Morgan says: stop, use simpler explanations → Morgan's canon becomes the methodological foundation of comparative psychology → but Morgan also believes in emergent evolution: some human capacities are genuinely new → later behaviorists misuse Morgan's canon to deny animal AND human consciousness.

**Key terms to define:** instinct, critical learning period, imprinting, introspection by analogy, Morgan's canon, emergent evolutionism, stimulus-response psychology

**Research anchors (to search):**
- Costall, A. (1993). "How Lloyd Morgan's Canon Backfired" — on the ironic legacy of Morgan's canon
