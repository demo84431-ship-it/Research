## Module Plan (5 Modules)

### Module 1: The Positivist Promise — Comte, Mill, and the Dream of a Social Science
**Source pages:** 217–221 | **Source words:** ~4,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | 19th-century context — industrialization, repression of naturalistic psychology, secularization | Opening |
| 2 | Comte's positivism — law of three stages (theological, metaphysical, positive), sociology as queen science | Positivism |
| 3 | Dogmatic empiricism — science as correlation of observables, no knowledge of real causes | Positivism |
| 4 | Mill's methods — agreement, difference, concomitant variation; psychology as inexact science; ethology | J.S. Mill |
| 5 | Mental chemistry — complex ideas as emergent, not merely aggregative | J.S. Mill |

**Narrative arc:** The 19th century opens with industrial progress and political repression → naturalistic psychology is dangerous → Comte offers positivism as a safe alternative: study correlations, not causes → Mill agrees but pushes further: here are the methods → but psychology is bound to remain inexact → and some ideas are more like chemical compounds than mechanical assemblies.

**Key terms to define:** positivism, law of three stages, dogmatic empiricism, methodological empiricism, Mill's methods, mental chemistry, ethology, parallelism

**Source pages:** 221–228 | **Source words:** ~7,600 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | James Mill — points of consciousness, utilitarianism, association and morality | James Mill |
| 2 | J.S. Mill's unconscious inference — Bailey debate, rational unconscious, ampliative inference | J.S. Mill |

**Key terms to define:** points of consciousness, unconscious inference, ampliative inference, rational unconscious, voluntary behavior, spontaneous nervous discharge, Spencer-Bain principle, conditioned reflexes

### Module 3: Reading Bumps, Mapping Brains — Phrenology and the Fight Over Localization
**Source pages:** 228–240 | **Source words:** ~8,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Hall's reflex arc — sensory nerve, spinal interconnection, motor nerve; excitory-motor vs. sensory-volitional | Hall |
| 2 | Gall's phrenology — cranioscopy, 27 faculties, comparative method, empirical biological psychology | Gall |
| 3 | Flourens's experimental ablation — cerebellum, medulla oblongata, functional unity of cortex, dualism defense | Flourens |
| 4 | Bell-Magendie law — separate sensory and motor nerve roots in spinal cord | Magendie |
| 5 | Broca, Fritsch & Hitzig — aphasia, cortical excitability, motor cortex, triumph of localization | Broca/Fritsch/Hitzig |

**Narrative arc:** Hall discovers the reflex arc → Gall reads bumps on skulls and claims to map the mind → the scientific establishment laughs (but Gall pioneered empirical methods) → Flourens fights back with ablation, defending the unity of the cortex (and the soul) → Magendie separates sensory from motor nerves → Broca finds that a specific brain region controls speech → Fritsch and Hitzig electrically stimulate the cortex and prove it's excitable → the localization debate is won.

**Key terms to define:** reflex arc, phrenology, cranioscopy, ablation, Bell-Magendie law, Broca's area, cortical excitability, neurophysiological dualism

### Module 4: The Nervous System as Machine — Müller, Helmholtz, Sechenov, and Fechner
**Source pages:** 240–256 | **Source words:** ~12,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Sensory-motor theory — Laycock, Jackson, the whole nervous system as reflexive | Sensory-motor theory |
| 2 | Müller, vitalism, and the Berlin Physical Society — specific energies, the oath against vitalism, du Bois-Reymond's electrophysiology | Müller/Berlin Physical Society |
| 3 | Helmholtz — conservation of energy, neural conduction speed, unconscious inference in perception, Young-Helmholtz color theory | Helmholtz |
| 4 | Sechenov's inhibition — Reflexes of the Brain, thought as the first two-thirds of a reflex, all behavior as reflexive | Sechenov |
| 5 | Fechner's psychophysics — mind-body identity, S = k log R, just noticeable differences, the bridge to experimental psychology | Fechner |

**Narrative arc:** The sensory-motor theory says the whole nervous system is reflexive → Müller teaches the generation that will transform physiology → his students swear an oath against vitalism → du Bois-Reymond proves nerves conduct electricity → Helmholtz measures the speed of neural conduction and develops unconscious inference → Sechenov discovers inhibition and declares all behavior is reflexive → Fechner measures the relationship between physical stimuli and mental sensation, creating psychophysics → the bridge between physiology and psychology is built.

**Key terms to define:** sensory-motor theory, specific energies, vitalism, Berlin Physical Society, conservation of energy, unconscious inference, inhibition, psychophysics, Fechner's law

### Module 5: The Birth of a Discipline — Physiological Psychology vs. Objective Psychology
**Source pages:** 256–257 | **Source words:** ~1,600 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Wundt's physiological psychology — experimental methods of physiology, but autonomous psychological explanation | Physiological psychology |
| 2 | Sechenov's objective psychology — physiological explanatory concepts applied to psychology | Objective psychology |
| 3 | Animal psychology as foundation — strong continuity between human and animal psychology | Sechenov |
| 4 | Epiphenomenalism — Huxley's conscious automata, consciousness as steam whistle | Huxley |
| 5 | Ideomotor behavior and unconscious cerebration — Carpenter, ideas generating behavior automatically | Carpenter |

**Narrative arc:** Two paths diverge → Wundt says: use physiology's methods but keep psychology's explanations → Sechenov says: use physiology's methods AND explanations → the study of animals should be the foundation → but wait — is consciousness even necessary? Huxley says it's just a steam whistle → Carpenter says ideas can generate behavior without the will → the stage is set for the battle between introspectionism and behaviorism.

**Key terms to define:** physiological psychology, objective psychology, epiphenomenalism, conscious automata, ideomotor behavior, unconscious cerebration
