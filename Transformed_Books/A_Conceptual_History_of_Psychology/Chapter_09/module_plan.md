| # | Concept | Source |

**Key terms to define:** Morrill Act, Scottish common sense psychology, pragmatism, new psychology, applied psychology

**Research anchors:**

**Source lines:** 1589–2052 | **Source words:** ~7,000 | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Titchener biography — Oxford, Leipzig, Cornell, authoritarian style | Titchener biography |

**Narrative arc:** Titchener translates Wundt, goes to Cornell, builds the largest PhD program in America → he creates structural psychology: find the atoms of consciousness → his method: systematic experimental introspection → but the Würzburg school finds thoughts without images → Titchener cannot replicate their results → the debate reveals that introspection produces different results in different labs → structural psychology dies with Titchener in 1927.

**Key terms to define:** structural psychology, conscious elements, systematic experimental introspection, stimulus error, imageless thought, Experimentalists

### Module 5: The Fight for Psychology's Soul — Structure vs. Function
**Source lines:** 2053–2085 + synthesis | **Source words:** ~600 + synthesis | **Concepts:** 5

| # | Concept | Source |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Structural vs. functional psychology — Titchener's distinction, what mind is vs. what mind does | Structural/functional debate |
| 2 | Dewey's functionalism — Chicago school, "The Reflex Arc Concept in Psychology" (1896) | Dewey |
| 3 | Angell's functionalism — Chicago department, habit, adjustment, practical orientation | Angell |
| 4 | Watson's behaviorism — "Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It" (1913), rejection of introspection | Watson |
| 5 | APA growth — from 31 charter members to 1,113, eclecticism wins, applied psychology dominates | APA development |

**Narrative arc:** Titchener distinguishes structural from functional psychology → Dewey argues the mind is for doing, not being → Angell builds the Chicago school around this idea → Watson takes it further: if the mind is for doing, study the doing and forget the mind → by 1913, introspection is dead and behaviorism is born → the APA grows from 31 members to over 1,000 → American psychology becomes what Wundt feared: applied, eclectic, and practical.

**Key terms to define:** functional psychology, structural psychology, reflex arc concept, behaviorism, adjustment

**Research anchors:**
- Dewey, J. (1896). "The Reflex Arc Concept in Psychology" — foundational functionalist paper
- Watson, J. B. (1913). "Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It" — behaviorist manifesto
